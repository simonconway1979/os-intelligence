Why it exists

Your team uses AI every day. None of it adds up.

The problem

Everyone uses AI their own way

One PM keeps a folder of prompts, another pastes notes into a chat, a third has a private setup nobody else can see. What each of them learns stays in their own chats, and none of it draws on what the company already knows.

So plans reach review unchallenged, research gets repeated because nobody can find the last round, and leaders see AI use going up without seeing what it changed.

The solution

One shared place for the team's knowledge

OS-Intelligence is a shared folder the whole product team works in, with AI built in. Meetings, documents and decisions go in. The AI turns them into one current picture of each project, and every point links back to where it came from.

Everyone works from that same picture, in the AI tools they already use.

What your team gets

Better answers, and plans that hold up

  • Answers built on what your company knows, with the source for each point.
  • An AI that challenges a plan with the evidence instead of telling you it looks great.
  • Research that's saved where the next person can find it.
  • Plans that stay in the PM's own words, so the PM still owns the decision.

In the field

Used with a product team.

June to September 2026, at a PE-backed European marketplace, I applied OS-Intelligence to the product organisation as a team operating system.

Idea to production. A seven-week pilot took two roadmap features from a one-line idea to an evidence-backed PRD, a working prototype and a live release, with product, design and engineering setting the quality bar.

Research agent with sourced claims. An agent retrieved and synthesised company knowledge and wrote every finding as a sourced claim with a quote and a link. A PM ran it by hand and asked for the prompts.

Evals

Tested, and the results reported as they came out.

Before claiming anything worked, I measured it against simpler setups: up to 21 questions with known answers, a control page sealed before any runs, and setups compared side by side. Losses included.

TestResult
Context comparison: the knowledge layer against a plain baselineMixed
Names the right people and says what it couldn't findHeld
Facts survive when the agent writes a summaryDidn't hold: two facts lost
Knowledge prepared locally beats leaving it in company toolsHeld, and a re-test confirmed it

The knowledge layer shows promise, but it didn't win on every measure. Next: more and larger datasets, starting with a public synthetic one that anyone can rerun.

Under the hood

How it's built.

V1 · open source · May 2026

A context layer in your repo

  • Skills loaded on demand. Each workflow is a Claude Code skill, so the agent only loads what the task needs.
  • Current-state synthesis. Meetings, PRDs, chats and notes become one current picture per project: position, decisions, people, open questions.
  • Agent memory in files. Sessions are saved as plain files in the repo, so the next session starts where the last one stopped.
  • Staleness flags. Context that hasn't been checked against a source for a while is marked, not trusted.
  • Sub-agent fan-out. Long synthesis splits across sub-agents and reconverges.
  • Eval harness. Answers scored against a known answer set.
V2 · in build · coming soon

A team operating system

  • One repo for the team. Every PM completes work, saves their session and runs research in the same place.
  • Hypothesis store. One agent keeps the team's open bets in date order, each with verbatim evidence for and against.
  • Decision-maker rule. A view about how a stakeholder judges success only weakens on that person's own dated words.
  • Two tracks, never merged. The agent's read (evidence only) sits beside the PM's own view and what would change their mind.
  • Conversation partner. Raises the few issues worth discussing and ends each one with a plan in the PM's words.
  • Research agent. Desk research on the riskiest assumptions, starting from "what if this is false", with a hard cap.
  • Meeting module. Preps a meeting from a plan, then reviews the transcript and feeds the store.
  • Save sweep. One command saves the session; background agents then update the shared state.
